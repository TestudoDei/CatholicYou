# CatholicYou editorial story arcs

Story arcs help the complete saint profiles reflect the real shape of a life instead of repeating one biography template. They are editorial guides, not claims about a saint's interior life and not substitutes for sources.

## Selection rules

1. Review the sources before choosing an arc.
2. Assign one primary arc. Add one secondary arc only when it materially clarifies the story.
3. Record a short, source-grounded rationale for the assignment.
4. Do not invent a weakness, conversion, conflict, quotation, or inner motive to create drama.
5. Do not force a neat resolution when the documented life contains prolonged or unresolved suffering.
6. Adjust section headings and emphasis to the individual saint; the sequence is a guide, not a fill-in-the-blanks script.
7. Reconsider the arc during human review if the story feels generic or the evidence points elsewhere.

## Shared foundation

Every complete story should include:

- clear historical or scriptural grounding;
- a distinction between documented history and later tradition;
- genuine spiritual movement supported by the record;
- the saint's enduring relevance;
- an invitation to prayer; and
- sources and editorial review status.

## Correction and transformation

**Central question:** How did grace correct, purify, or redirect this person's gifts?

Suggested sequence:

1. Origin and calling
2. Early gifts or privileged witness
3. Documented misunderstanding, weakness, or misdirected desire
4. Correction and formation
5. Mature or costly fidelity
6. Enduring legacy and invitation to prayer

Use only when the correction or change is documented. Saint James is the initial example: privileged witness, ambition corrected by Christ, later fidelity unto martyrdom, and an enduring pilgrim legacy.

## Conversion

**Central question:** What changed when this person encountered and responded to grace?

Suggested sequence:

1. Life before conversion
2. Search, resistance, or disordered desire
3. Encounter or turning point
4. Response and changed life
5. Fruit of conversion
6. Enduring legacy and invitation to prayer

## Perseverance

**Central question:** How did this person remain faithful through a prolonged trial?

Suggested sequence:

1. Origin and vocation
2. The hope or responsibility entrusted to the person
3. Prolonged trial
4. Practices of faithful perseverance
5. Outcome, including unresolved suffering when applicable
6. Enduring legacy and invitation to prayer

## Courageous witness

**Central question:** How did this person bear witness when fidelity became dangerous or costly?

Suggested sequence:

1. Historical setting and vocation
2. The truth or community served
3. Pressure, opposition, or persecution
4. Response and testimony
5. Sacrifice, martyrdom, or aftermath
6. Enduring legacy and invitation to prayer

Avoid sensationalizing violence. The emphasis belongs on the saint's witness and fidelity.

## Hidden faithfulness

**Central question:** How did ordinary, largely unseen fidelity become spiritually fruitful?

Suggested sequence:

1. Origin and ordinary vocation
2. Daily practices of faithfulness
3. Unseen sacrifice or difficulty
4. Growth in holiness
5. Spiritual fruit
6. Enduring legacy and invitation to prayer

## Service and self-gift

**Central question:** How did responding to another person's need deepen into a life of self-gift?

Suggested sequence:

1. Origin and vocation
2. A need encountered
3. Initial response
4. Deepening commitment and personal cost
5. Lasting work, community, or example
6. Enduring legacy and invitation to prayer

## Intellectual or spiritual discovery

**Central question:** How did a sustained search for truth deepen this person's life and teaching?

Suggested sequence:

1. Origin and central question
2. Search, study, or spiritual longing
3. Discovery, insight, or encounter
4. Teaching and lived response
5. Influence, limits, and historical context
6. Enduring legacy and invitation to prayer

Avoid reducing a thinker or mystic to ideas alone; show how insight shaped a lived vocation.
